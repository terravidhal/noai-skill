# Direction — five lines before the first edit

A cleaned-up screen without a direction becomes the next template: grey page,
Inter, one accent, the same card grid. The direction is the decision that stops
that. It is short, concrete and derived from the product in front of you.

Write it before editing. Show it to the user on a full correction (score ≥ 45)
and wait, unless they already said "just do it". Keep it in the report either way.

## The five lines

### 1. Product

The job it performs and the people it serves, in a single plain sentence, using
the nouns already on the page. Not the slogan. Not the category.

> A project workspace for three-person design studios that turns hours into an
> invoice.

### 2. Character

Two or three specific qualities that could contradict each other.

> `calm, exact, a little warm` · `fast, technical, dry` · `patient, editorial`

Forbidden, because they describe every template: beautiful, elegant, innovative,
premium, sleek, modern, clean, minimal, cutting-edge, futuristic.

### 3. World

The place or material the product belongs to. This world decides how the neutral
ramp, the accent and the visual are tinted.

| Product | World | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Note app | paper, ink, desk light | warm off-white, graphite, one ink accent |
| Monitoring | instruments, night shift | ink ramp, cool accent, mono figures |
| Travel | maps, window light | airy neutral, one sky or terrain accent |
| Finance | receipts, ledgers, card stock | warm grey, green only for money |
| Teaching | notebooks, red pen | paper ramp, restrained red accent |

A dashboard can use the world in the chart and the one moment, not in the
surfaces. A landing page may tint its surfaces further toward the world.

### 4. Moments

One or two places where the screen is allowed to be rich: a real chart, a
rendered object, a product preview, a photograph with its own light. Say what
they are and where they sit.

> The six-month revenue line, and the invoice preview in the hero. Everything
> else stays neutral.

Slop spreads colour across the chrome. Craft concentrates it in one or two
content moments.

### 5. Signature

A single distinctive mark that belongs to this product, shown at most twice —
never five times.

> A 1 px baseline under the active project name · a keycap on every shortcut ·
> a route line between milestones · a stamped `paid` mark on a settled invoice.

The signature is not a new decoration. It should come from how the product
actually works.

## Keep a real brand

When a real logo, colour or typeface already exists and is not itself slop,
preserve it and build around it. A purple-to-blue gradient with sparkles is slop
even when it sits in the logo, unless the user says the gradient is the brand.

Do not rename the product. Do not redraw a real logo. If the logo itself is an
emoji or a glossy orb, replace it with a flat wordmark or a simple glyph in one
solid colour, and say so in the report.

## The template test

Replace the product name with a competitor's. If the headline, the visual and the
page still make sense, the direction is too weak. Push on the noun, the moment
and the signature until the swap breaks.

## Worked examples

### Weak

- Product: "The all-in-one platform for modern teams."
- Character: "clean, modern, premium"
- World: "the cloud"
- Moments: "the gradient hero"
- Signature: "sparkles"

This could be anything. It will produce the same screen as the last run.

### Strong

- Product: "Invoice and hours for a three-person design studio."
- Character: "calm, exact, a little warm"
- World: "paper, ledger, desk light"
- Moments: "the invoice total in the hero; the revenue line on the overview"
- Signature: "a thin stamped rule under a settled invoice"

The nouns are specific, the palette follows the world, and the signature comes
from the work the product does.

## When the user already has a brand

Ask for the logo file, the colour and the typeface if they exist. Build the
direction around them. Do not invent a new brand to avoid the work, and do not
keep a gradient simply because it is in the current logo — unless the user
confirms it is intentional.
